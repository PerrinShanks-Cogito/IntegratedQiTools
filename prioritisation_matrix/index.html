Build a prioritisation matrix

Score each change idea for how easy it is to do and how much impact it would have, and see them on a 2x2 matrix so you can pick the quick wins to test first. Use Data sheet to download a blank sheet for your team to score ideas in. Everything runs in your browser, so your data is never uploaded.

Your ideas

One idea per row: the idea in the first column, then its ease score and its impact score. With column headings, the columns named Ease and Impact are used, in any order, and any others (such as notes or owner) are ignored.

Matrix settings

Scoring scale

Worked out from your scores.

Scores at or above the split count as high. Leave blank to split at the middle of the scale.

Result

Paste your scored ideas on the left to build your matrix.

How to read it: each dot is one idea, placed by how easy it is to do (left to right) and how much difference it would make (bottom to top). Ideas in the top right are quick wins: test these first. Top left are major projects, worth doing but they need planning and support. Bottom right are fill-ins to pick up if time allows, and bottom left are thankless tasks, best dropped or rethought.

Scoring well: agree as a team what a low and a high score mean before you start, and score together or each separately and then talk through big differences. Ease covers time, cost, staff and how much the change is within your control. Impact is how much it would move your aim. The scores are judgments, so use the matrix to guide the conversation rather than to settle it, and test ideas small with PDSA cycles.
