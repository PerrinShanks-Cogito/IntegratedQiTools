Build a tree plot

Paste your data and see how a total breaks down into groups and the items within them, each drawn as a box sized by its share. Use Data sheet to download a spreadsheet to collect your data in. Everything runs in your browser, so your data is never uploaded.

Your data

One column for each level, from the broadest group to the item (up to three, such as directorate, ward and reason), then a column of counts or values. Leave out the numbers to count one for each row instead.

Chart settings

Items smaller than this share of the total are put together as "Other" within their group. Use 0 to show them all.

Result

Paste data on the left to build your chart.

How to read it: a tree plot (also called a treemap) shows a total as one rectangle split into boxes. Each box's area is its share of the total, so the biggest boxes are where most of the count sits. Boxes of the same colour belong to the same group, with the group's name in the band across its top, so you can see both which groups are largest and what makes up each one.

When to use it: when your data has a hierarchy, such as incidents by directorate, ward and type, or delays by site and reason, and you want to see where to focus. To rank one level and find the vital few, use a Pareto chart; to compare a few categories exactly, a bar chart is easier to read, because people judge lengths better than areas.
