T chart for rare events

Paste the date of each rare event, such as a never event, a fall with harm or a hospital-acquired infection, and see whether the time between events is getting longer or shorter. Use it when events happen too seldom for an ordinary SPC chart, so most weeks or months would show a zero.

Calculated in your browser. Nothing you paste is uploaded or saved.

Event dates

One event per line, oldest first, as day/month/year (03/01/2024), 2024-01-03 or 3 Jan 2024. Anything after the date, such as a second column, is shown as a note on the chart. A header row is fine.

About the chart

For harm, longer gaps between events are better. Choose shorter gaps for events you want more often.

Process limits

To recalculate limits after a known change to the process, click that point on the chart, or use the table of values.

Axes

Special cause rules

A single point outside the limits is always a signal. A shift of 6 points follows NHS England's rare events T chart; choose 7 if you prefer.

Days between rare events

How the T chart works

Each point is the number of days since the previous event, plotted at the date of the event. The first event starts the clock, so it has no point. Two events on the same day count as half a day apart.

Times between events are skewed: most gaps are short and a few are very long. So the tool converts each gap to its power of 1/3.6 (about 0.2777), which makes the values close to normally distributed. It works out the mean and the average moving range of the converted values, sets the limits at the mean ± 2.66 × the average moving range, and converts the mean and limits back to days with a power of 3.6. When the converted lower limit falls below zero there is no lower limit.

A point above the upper limit is an unusually long gap, so events have become less frequent. A point below the lower limit, or a run of points below the mean, means events are closer together than expected. Points that are inside the limits are worth a look too if they form an unusual pattern, or if far more or fewer than two thirds of them sit in the middle third between the limits.

Only recalculate the limits where there is a known change to the process and the data show a sustained shift afterwards. Based on NHS England's T chart rare events SPC tool (version 2.0).
