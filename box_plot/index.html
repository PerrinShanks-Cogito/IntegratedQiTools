Build a box and whisker plot

Paste your measurements and compare their middle, spread and unusual values across groups, such as waiting times by site or day. Everything runs in your browser, so your data is never uploaded.

Your data

Either two columns, a group name and a value, with one row for each measurement; or one column of values for each group, with the group names in the first row. A single column of numbers gives one box.

Chart settings

The usual choice marks values more than 1.5 times the box height beyond the box as outliers, drawn as separate circles.

Result

Paste data on the left to build your chart.

How to read it: each box holds the middle half of the values for one group. The line across the box is the median, so half the values are above it and half below. The box runs from the lower quartile (a quarter of values are below it) to the upper quartile (a quarter are above it); its height is the interquartile range. The whiskers reach out to the rest of the values, and any outliers, values more than 1.5 times the box height beyond the box, are drawn as separate circles.

When to use it: to compare the spread of a measure across several groups at once, such as waiting times by site, length of stay by ward or time to treatment by shift. A tall box or long whiskers means the process is less predictable; a box that sits off-centre around its median shows the values are skewed. A box plot doesn't show change over time, so use a run chart or SPC chart for that, and a gap between two boxes isn't proof of a real difference, particularly when a group has only a few values. Quartiles are worked out the same way as Excel's QUARTILE.INC.
