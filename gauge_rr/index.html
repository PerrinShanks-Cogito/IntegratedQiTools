Check a measurement system (Gauge R&R)

Paste the results of a study where several people (appraisers) each measured the same items more than once, and see how much of the variation comes from the measuring itself rather than from real differences between the items. Use Data sheet to download a spreadsheet to record the study in. Everything runs in your browser, so your data is never uploaded.

Your data

Either three columns, the part (the item measured), the appraiser and the measurement, with one row for each measurement; or a table with the appraiser and the trial in the first two columns and one column for each part. Every appraiser should measure every part the same number of times, at least twice.

Result

What it does: a Gauge repeatability and reproducibility (R&R) study splits the variation in your measurements into what comes from the items themselves (part to part) and what comes from measuring them. Measurement variation has two parts: repeatability, how much one person’s readings of the same item vary, and reproducibility, how much different people’s readings differ. If measuring adds a lot of variation, changes in your data could be noise from the measurement rather than real change.

Reading the results: the total Gauge R&R as a percentage of study variation (or of the known process variation, if you give it) shows whether the measurement can track improvement: under 10% is excellent, 10% to 30% is generally acceptable and over 30% is not good enough. The percentage of tolerance shows whether it can tell good items from bad, on the same scale. The number of distinct categories is how many groups the measurement can reliably split the items into; 5 or more is the usual minimum.

Running a study: choose about 10 items that cover the range you normally see, picked at random from everyday work, and two or three people who usually take the measurement. Each person measures every item two or three times, in a random order, without seeing their earlier results. Fix the measurement first if it fails, for example with clearer instructions, training or better equipment, and then repeat the study.
