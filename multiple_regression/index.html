What it does: multiple regression fits the straight-line equation that best predicts one result from several factors at once. Each factor's coefficient is the change in the result for a one-unit rise in that factor with the other factors held steady, so it separates the effect of, say, staffing from the effect of how busy the department was. Text factors are compared against a reference category.

Before you act: regression shows association, not cause and effect. A factor you didn't measure may be behind the pattern. Use it to choose which change ideas to test with PDSA cycles, check the result is stable on an SPC chart first, and aim for at least 10 rows for every factor in the model.
