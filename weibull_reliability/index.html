Weibull reliability analysis

Paste how long each item lasted before it failed, such as days until a piece of equipment broke down, and fit a Weibull distribution. See how likely things are to keep working over time, when they tend to fail, and whether failures speed up with age. Everything runs in your browser, so your data is never uploaded.

One time per row, from Excel or a CSV: for example hours, days or cycles until each item failed. Use the same unit throughout.

Result

What it does: the Weibull distribution describes how long things last before they fail. Its shape tells you how the risk of failure changes with age, and its scale is the time by which about 63% of items have failed (the characteristic life). A threshold, in the 3-parameter model, shifts the whole distribution to start at a minimum life.

Reading the shape: a shape below 1 means the risk of failure falls with age, so failures happen early, often from faults in manufacture, installation or set-up. A shape close to 1 means failures happen at random, whatever the age. A shape above 1 means the risk rises with age, as items wear out, so planned replacement or maintenance before that point can prevent failures.

Checking the fit: if the Weibull distribution fits, the points on the probability plot lie close to a straight line. A curve bending down at the start often means a threshold is needed. The shape and scale are maximum likelihood estimates, which take account of items still working when the study stopped. The probability plot uses median ranks of the failures out of all items.
