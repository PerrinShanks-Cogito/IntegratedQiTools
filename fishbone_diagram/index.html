Build a cause and effect diagram

Also called a fishbone or Ishikawa diagram. The effect sits at the head and the causes branch off the bones.

This goes in the box at the head of the fish, on the right.

Categories and causes

Choosing a set renames your categories in order and keeps their causes.

Most sets use five or six categories. Categories alternate above and below the spine, from left to right.

Press Enter in a box to add the next cause at the same level. Shift+Enter starts a new line.

A cause and effect diagram helps a team explore and display the many causes that contribute to an effect or outcome. It shows how the causes relate to the effect and to each other, which helps you find the changes worth testing.

It is also called an Ishikawa diagram, after its creator, or a fishbone diagram, because it looks like the bones of a fish.

How to build one

  1. Write the effect you want to influence. The tool puts it in a box on the right-hand side.
  2. The spine is drawn for you: a horizontal line running left from the effect.
  3. Choose five or six categories of causes. The classic categories are Materials, Methods, Equipment, Environment and People, so a new diagram starts with these. Use Category set to switch to SEIPS, 6Ms, 4Ps, Functions or Process steps, or choose Custom to name your own. Rename, add or remove them to suit your problem.
  4. Each category becomes a bone: a diagonal line above or below the spine, with the category name in a box at its end.
  5. List the causes in each category that contribute to the effect. Each cause is drawn as a branch bone. Use Ask “Why?” to add sub-causes branching off a cause.
Tip: develop the causes by asking “Why?” until you reach a useful level of detail, when a cause is specific enough to test a change and measure its effects.

Next steps

  • Build the diagram with people who know the process well.
  • Count how often the main causes happen with a Pareto chart, to focus on the vital few.
  • Turn the causes you want to tackle into drivers and change ideas in a driver diagram.

Edit as outline

Put each category on its own line with no indent. Indent causes beneath it with two spaces, and sub-causes with two more. Use “Title:” and “Effect:” for the title and effect.
