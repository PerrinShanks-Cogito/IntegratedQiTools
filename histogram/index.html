Build a histogram

Paste your measurements and see how they are spread out: where most values sit, how wide the spread is, and whether there is a long tail. Everything runs in your browser, so your data is never uploaded.

Your data

One number per row, such as waiting times or lengths of stay. If you paste several columns, choose which one to plot.

Chart settings

Automatic picks a round bin width that suits the amount of data. A value on a bin edge is counted in the bin above it.

Optional. Add a target or standard (for example, triage within 15 minutes) to see how many values fall outside it.

Result

Paste data on the left to build your chart.

How to read it: each bar covers a range of values, and its height shows how many of your measurements fall in that range. Look at where the tallest bars sit (the typical value), how wide the bars spread (the variation), and whether one side has a long tail. Two separate peaks can mean two different processes are mixed together, such as weekdays and weekends.

Normality test: the Anderson–Darling test checks whether your data could reasonably come from a normal (bell-shaped) distribution. A p-value below 0.05 suggests they do not. Small samples rarely fail the test, and very large samples fail it for tiny departures, so always look at the chart as well.

A histogram shows the overall shape but not change over time. To see whether a process is changing, plot the same data in time order on a run chart or SPC chart.
